# Solid solutions & intermediates

## Substitutional solid solutions

Sites of solvent metals are occupied by single metal atoms (e.g. brass is made of copper sites, zinc atoms between them)

Solubility - the property of a metal that defines how much other metal can be mixed with the former without crystalization (rozpuszczalność). Limited solubility causes crystallization (przesycenie)

2 elements can have unlimited solubility when they have identical electropositive or unit cell properties.

## Interstitial solid soutions

Interstitial alloys are solid solutions where small atoms occupy space between larger atoms of metal (e.g. steel - carbon occupies the 'holes' between iron atoms)

## Intermediate phases

Where solubility limit is exceeded, the metal enters an intermediate phase and can crystallize. The crystalline structure is different of pure metal's.

Types of interm. phases:
> 1. metallic compounds (metal & nonmetal)
> 2. intermetallic compounds (metal & metal)

# Deformations

Enough force applied to a material causes a deformation. 

## Elastic deformation

Elastic deformation causes the material to return to its original shape.

## Plastic deformation

The deformation is permanent. Types:

> slip
> twinning

1. Twinning - Shear force causes atoms to mirror each other (the position is mirrored on a plane called a twin boundary)

Dislocation of atoms = cause of deformation

Dislocating motion is the easiest in metals in comparison to other materials, because the bonding is non-directional.

Mixed dislocations - both perpendicular & parallel dislocations

2. Slip - Possesses a slip system. 

Two planes shift in the direction of highest linear density.

Work hardening - after being deformed, a metal becomes stronger and resistant to new deformations.

Recrystallization - a hardened material can bloom new crystals, which are weaker and cause the hardened material to return to initial strength. 

## Cold work

Deformations in room temperature.

> forging
> drawing
> rolling
> extrusion

%CW (cold work) = [(A0-Ad)/A0]x100

# Classification of metals, ceramics and alloys

> ferrous (mostly iron)
> nonferrous (mostly non-iron)

> SAE

First 2 numbers:

> 12 - resulfurized & rephosphorized

> 31 - Ni 1.25% Cr 0.65-0.80%

> 44 - Molybdenum Steel Mo 0.4-0.52%

## Stainless steel

Cr -> Cr2O3 oxide protects the metal from rust. Fe with >11 wt% Cr = stainless steel

## Cast irons

> white (Fe + Fe3C)
> grey (Fe + C)

Ductile (nodular) iron = has properties of white and grey iron, achieved through a manufacturing process

Grey iron - has Si atoms

Properties of grey & white cast iron are dependent on C or Fe3C structure.

## Ceramics

Invited early, importance increasing since 1970s

Ceramics vs. glasses

Ceramics: Inorganic, nonmetallic, **crystalline**

Glasses: Inorganic, nonmetallic, **no crystal structure**
> normally used in low-tech
> in the future, high-tech (e.g. fiber optics)

Ceramics have ionic or covalent bonds. THey consist of **oxides, nitrides, carbides**

> hard
> brittle
> strong ionic bonds
> resistant to high temperature and corrosion
> no free electrons
> electrical isolators

Classification:
> natural (e.g. rocks)
> industrial (e.g. cmeent)
> advanced (e.g. cutting tools)

Processing techniques:
> low-temp setting - concrete
> melting - glass
> paste (slip) shaping, then firing - bricks
> sintering
> hot isostatic pressing (HIP)
> vitrification

Common ceramic components:
> alumina (Al2O3)
> silicon carbide (SiC)
> silicon nitride (Si3N4)
> zirconia (ZrO2)
> sialons (Si3Al3O3N5)
> glasses
